\paragraph{Structural boundary of delivery identity.}
Delivery identity is a sufficient condition for matched control histories when weights, the initial policy/environment state, cache transformations and exogenous randomness agree. Inductively, equal sender-keyed payloads and source timestamps give the same receiver inputs and actions, which preserve the next environment and policy states. This is a deterministic consistency argument rather than a new communication-learning result. In the present serial model, Full orders senders from $t\bmod N$, whereas RR begins at its cursor and advances by admitted count. With exactly one successful admission per step and cursor zero at reset, both deliver $t\bmod N$. If both instead deliver a constant $1<k<N$ consecutive senders without admission failures, their start indices are $t$ and $kt$ modulo $N$; equal sets require $(k-1)t=0\pmod N$. Equal packet counts therefore do not suffice.

\paragraph{Three observations on one frozen-policy cohort.}
The retained identity campaign permits traffic, behavior and matched-history conclusions on the same ten Full-trained checkpoints, twenty resets and two matched decay settings. At Slow/5 ms, RR reduces offered bytes per step by 80\% and preserves all 400 sender, cache, action and return histories. At Medium/2 ms, RR reduces offered bytes per step by 60\% and delivers the same count as Full on every common step, yet sender identities first differ at $t=1$ in all 400 pairs. Full episode count histories coincide in 349 pairs because some episode durations diverge. Return remains equal in 259 pairs, including 222 with different action histories. Thus the aggregate saving and maintained return can coexist with changed control behavior even within the same frozen-policy cohort; the Slow identity result should be read as the order-aligned, one-packet case, not a generic property of capacity capping. Offered bytes are charged requests, not a measurement of serialized radio bytes or energy.

\paragraph{Common history and readiness boundaries.}
The common RR prefix equalizes the entire joint history at branching, including environment state, recurrent state, cached information and scheduler state. Its zero Slow return contrast (400 pairs), despite 126 differing sender traces and 94 differing action traces on common suffix steps, cannot uniquely assign the attenuation to cache initialization. At a 2.25 ms deadline the three-packet preparation threshold is 310 microseconds. Episode-first, seed-equal RR delivery averages are 2.2060, 2.0048 and 2.0024 packets in the three repeat processes, against 2.0000 for DAC. Identical-input snapshot comparisons retain extra DAC gate costs of 98.2, 99.7 and 100.3 microseconds under that same weighting, but no snapshot delivery-count difference. These results separate a repeated computational overhead from its process-dependent conversion into missed reception opportunities; process repetitions are not extra training seeds.
