# Current claims — KBS manuscript, 2026-11-23 KST

**Evaluating Deadline-Limited Communication in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Delivery, Computation, and Control**

Canonical source: [English manuscript](experiments/deadline_comm/publication/kbs/kbs_main.tex), [PDF](experiments/deadline_comm/publication/kbs/kbs_main.pdf), [version and validation status](experiments/deadline_comm/publication/kbs/README.md). The November 23 revision includes original-trace boundary analyses, paired fresh receiver-head rollouts and a learned external MNIST-sum case. The primary deployment estimand weights episodes equally within seed, then weights seeds equally.

## Contribution

A controlled evaluation framework attributes communication-efficiency outcomes to admission, timely delivery, sender selection, initialization, computation and downstream policy use. Matched-checkpoint delivery-identity tests, feasible same-count sender substitutions, common-prefix interventions, measured/fixed readiness and receiver-input probes form a four-stage evaluation protocol. The contribution is their operational combination for deadline-and-cache evaluation, not a new communication-learning objective or a generally superior scheduler.

## Supported findings and boundaries

- **Delivery identity (matched delivery-history experiment):** Oracle-assisted five-agent Hallway, ten slow-trained Full checkpoints, 20 paired resets and two matched cache-decay settings: all 400 Full–RR paired episodes preserve delivered histories, caches, actions, rewards and return at fixed readiness, while RR reduces attempted traffic by 80%.
- **Sender effects (same-count subset experiment):** same-count substitutions change suffix return in approximately 13.9–18.9% of sampled states under the primary aggregation. Priority-over-uniform advantages depend on state versus episode weighting; all four equal-episode intervals include zero. Nonzero sender effects do not establish robust DAC superiority. After a common RR prefix, suffix return changes in 0/400 Slow and 15/400 Medium episodes; equal return can conceal different information/actions.
- **Computation and information use (timing and receiver studies):** selector readiness changes the reception opportunity. In Oracle-free custom broadcast with source-aware selection, held-out aggregate probes reach 99.91–100% accuracy versus 64.17–78.44% for unchanged acting policies. Accessible information is not fully exploited in those cells; the follow-up below tests a supervised change to the acting head. Broadcast passes the stated learning check, XOR fails (0/10).
- **Decision on receiver adaptation:** At normal RR access in Medium/2 ms broadcast, a 100-update labelled follower-head adaptation raised actual accuracy from 56.575% to 63.006% on 20 fresh resets for each of ten seeds. Paired gain +6.43 percentage points [3.32, 9.54]. This uses additional labels and includes closed-loop feedback. Source-aware adaptation gains +22.67 points but every source-aware step misses the charged action deadline in that diagnostic implementation.
- **Published communication task, learned reference and admission decision:** A paper-based reconstruction of Eccles et al. passed its learning check in all three both-bias seeds at 12,000 updates. On 2,048 test pairs per seed, sampled accuracy was 50.21% with both biases and 9.67% with neither. On a distinct 1,024-pair test, original communication-use measures and shuffle controls already show useful communication. At a modeled 750 µs cutoff, Full and timing-informed admission have identical action distributions at 17 and 8.5 offered B/episode. Against random admission at the same 8.5 B, timing knowledge increases expected accuracy by 10.59 percentage points [7.25, 13.92], across three seeds. This is a one-step single-speaker case with known exogenous readiness.
- **Direct receiver-memory check:** In 200 Slow/5 ms RR-checkpoint episodes, clearing cache at the common branch changed later action histories in 154 RR and 155 DAC pairs. All four branches had zero suffix return. This comparison isolates a receiver-memory reset at those endpoints and supplies no evidence for cache mediation of the historical return contrast.
- **Delivery boundary:** Medium/2 ms Full versus RR reduced bytes offered per step by 60%. Counts matched on every common step and sender IDs diverged at the second decision in all 400 pairs. Among 259 equal-return pairs, 222 had different action histories. Slow single-packet identity follows the aligned transmission order.
- **Task necessity (local-clock control):** a constructive local-distance/public-clock controller achieves return 20 without messages on all 1,024 Hallway starts. Learned Ideal–No-Comm gaps concern the specified Oracle-assisted training procedure, not task-level communication necessity.
- **SMACv2 feasibility (technical smoke):** 20 technical-smoke cells/episodes, 1,046 steps, one seed-0 checkpoint. Slow/5 ms fixed-readiness identity holds over one 47-step episode with 80% offered-wire-byte reduction. Medium/2 ms has a sender mismatch despite equal payload goodput. Shadow traces after mismatch are not independent realized return trajectories. The earlier pilot failed its learning gate. A separate Terran continuation passed the 300k learning milestone, then failed independent-engine replay at the second seed. Multi-seed SMACv2 deployment contrasts remain unavailable.
