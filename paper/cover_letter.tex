To the Editors of Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems,

Please consider our manuscript, ``Evaluating Deadline-Limited Communication in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Delivery, Computation, and Control'', as a Regular Paper.

The paper examines what communication-efficiency comparisons identify when actions have deadlines and received messages persist in memory. Matched-policy delivery comparisons, feasible sender substitutions, shared-history and receiver-memory interventions, measured readiness and acting-head interventions separate traffic admission, reception and control. The resulting four-stage protocol is supported by Oracle-assisted Hallway, Oracle-free broadcast and a reconstruction of a published MNIST-sum communication game.

Additional frozen-policy evaluations show when admission gains survive readiness uncertainty and when receiver improvements survive rejection of late actions. They retain the high-cost conditions that eliminate or reverse these gains. The manuscript reports both positive effects and null comparisons, and explicitly limits its claims to the studied policies and channels. It does not claim a generally superior scheduler or generalization to standard sequential MARL benchmarks. We believe its focus on evaluating communication and decision making is relevant to the journal's readership.

An Information Sheet and supplementary material accompany the manuscript. The authors report no prior conference or journal publication, no specific research funding and no competing interests. Code availability and reviewer-data access are described in the manuscript.
