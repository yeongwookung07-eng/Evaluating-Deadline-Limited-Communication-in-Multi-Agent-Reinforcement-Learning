To the Editors of Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems,

Please consider our manuscript, ``Evaluating Deadline-Limited Communication in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Delivery, Computation, and Control'', as a Regular Paper.

In the same five-agent Hallway policies, two access rules each save 80\% of offered bytes. One preserves every delivered update and action across 400 paired episodes. The other changes every sender history and lowers mean return when cached messages retain full weight. A traffic comparison alone would give these rules the same rating.

Our contribution is an evaluation procedure for identifying where such differences arise. It combines sender-keyed reception traces, replay of feasible alternatives from the same state, and fresh receiver rollouts with a deadline on the executed action. In separate broadcast and MNIST-sum studies we show how decision costs and receiver supervision affect the interpretation. All reported timings come from one host and a modeled channel. Section 5.8 extends sender substitution to SMACv2 Terran and native SchedNet Predator--Prey. Sender substitutions changed realized return at 27 of 30 Terran decisions and 22 of 139 Predator--Prey decisions. The factual choices had no resolved average advantage. We document the Terran replay fault, its repair and the independent no-op checks.

An Information Sheet and supplementary material accompany the manuscript. The authors report no prior conference or journal publication, no specific research funding and no competing interests. Code availability and reviewer-data access are described in the manuscript.
