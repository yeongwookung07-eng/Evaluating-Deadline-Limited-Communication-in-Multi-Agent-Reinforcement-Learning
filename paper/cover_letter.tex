To the Editors of Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems,

Please consider our manuscript, ``Evaluating Deadline-Limited Communication in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Delivery, Computation, and Control'', as a Regular Paper.

The paper asks what explains a communication-cost and performance difference when actions have deadlines and received messages persist. A traffic saving can remove offers that would not arrive; equal received counts can conceal different information; and an informative receiver input need not lead to a useful action. Distinguishing these possibilities matters for deciding whether to change admission, sender selection or the acting policy.

The analysis follows the observations and controls needed to distinguish those explanations. It uses matched-policy delivery traces, feasible same-count sender substitutions, shared-history and receiver-memory interventions, measured readiness and actual receiver-head rollouts. The manuscript states this reasoning before presenting the results, and organizes the completed comparisons into a four-stage evaluation protocol. Evidence comes from Oracle-assisted Hallway, Oracle-free broadcast and a reconstruction of the published MNIST-sum game.

Frozen-policy follow-ups test uncertainty in readiness and rejection of late actions. The paper retains the conditions that eliminate or reverse favorable effects, the unresolved priority and memory-effect comparisons, and the limitations of the tasks and timing model. Its contribution concerns the interpretation of communication and control in these evaluated settings. We believe this focus is relevant to the journal's readership.

An Information Sheet and supplementary material accompany the manuscript. The authors report no prior conference or journal publication, no specific research funding and no competing interests. Code availability and reviewer-data access are described in the manuscript.
